# #456: prefetching the next GEMV's head into the MALL during the decode's gaps — the bench says no

Engine main after #451 + the `#456` section in `bench/launch/chain_size.hip`, RX
9060 XT 16 GB, 2026-10-14. The bench is #420's chain of the step's real GEMV
sizes (257 segments, ~11 GiB) with a latency-bound FMA dummy between segments (the
decode's ~165 small kernels, ~2.5 ms of DRAM-idle time per step) and, on a side
stream, a cacheable read of the next segment's head:

    hipcc -O3 --offload-arch=gfx1200 -o /var/tmp/chain456 bench/launch/chain_size.hip && /var/tmp/chain456

## Two regimes

**With a 352 us gap** (the harness's first calibration, ~23x the real gap): the
prefetch has all the time it needs and the MALL *does* hold its bytes -

| prefetch | chain | GB/s |
|---|---:|---:|
| none | 126.10 ms | 84.6 |
| 2 MiB | **124.59 ms** | 85.6 |
| 4 MiB | 124.88 ms | 85.4 |
| 8 MiB | 126.06 ms | 84.6 |
| 16 MiB | 127.63 ms | 83.6 |
| 24 MiB | 130.35 ms | 81.8 |

2-4 MiB win ~1.2-1.5 ms per step's worth of chain and 16-24 MiB lose (the MALL is
32 MB and the segments' own streaming evicts the rest). That is the issue's "16-24
MB" idea at the size it can actually hold - and it is roughly the upper bound:
~1.3 ms of the ~3.8 ms a pure streaming chain loses to its cold starts.

**With the real 17 us gap** (calibrated to #203's ~15 us per small kernel), the
same prefetches all *lose*:

| prefetch | chain | GB/s | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 39.34 ms | 271.2 | |
| 2 MiB | 41.05 ms | 259.8 | +1.71 ms |
| 4 MiB | 41.74 ms | 255.5 | +2.40 ms |
| 8 MiB | 44.67 ms | 238.8 | +5.33 ms |
| 16 MiB | 50.74 ms | 210.2 | +11.40 ms |
| 24 MiB | 55.47 ms | 192.3 | +16.13 ms |

The 2 MiB row's +1.71 ms is exactly the prefetch's own DRAM time (2 MiB x 256
segments / 318 GB/s = 1.7 ms): with a 17 us window the prefetched bytes do not
survive to the next segment, so they are a second read of the same data. The
scaling with the size follows the same arithmetic, and even the *smallest* useful
prefetch pays a launch (~3.5-8 us) for a window worth 2-5 us of DRAM time: the
launch's fill/drain is the same order as the ramp it removes, 257 times per step.

## Decision

**Closed with the bench number**, as the issue's constraint allows. The MALL does
hold a small prefetched head when there is time (the first table), but the decode
step's real gaps are too short for the traffic to amortize: the prefetch's own
launch and bytes cost ~1.7 ms per step where the whole opportunity is ~1.3-3.8 ms,
and it measured *negative* at every size. No engine-side switch is added.
