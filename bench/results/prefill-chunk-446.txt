# #446: the prefill chunk (512 / 768 / 1024) — measured, 512 stays

Engine main after #445 + the `--chunk N` option on `omph-run` (added here: the
server had it, the runner did not, and the issue's step 3 needs its logits).
RX 9060 XT 16 GB, CachyOS, 2026-10-14, the card's settings unchanged (MCLK
1279 MHz, cap 182 W — see #447's comment).

Hypothesis to test (the issue): the 2x8-tile GEMM covers 256 W rows per
workgroup, 32 workgroups run at once, so the 5120-row shapes (ffn_down, the
attn/delta-net outputs: 20 x 4 = 80 workgroups) leave the third round half empty,
worth ~4-5 % of the prefill; a 1024-token chunk lands every shape on whole rounds.

## The matrix

`omph-server --chunk C --cache-ram 0 --ctx 131072`, the same 4096/16384/32768
token prompts, 5 runs per cell, the median of the prefill wall (`prompt_ms` of the
response) and the peak VRAM `rocm-smi` reports while the request runs:

| chunk | 4096 | 16384 | 32768 | peak VRAM |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **512** | **3934.0 ms (1041.2 t/s)** | 16801.9 (975.1) | 36188.9 (905.5) | **15157 MiB** |
| 768 | 4053.3 (1010.5) | 17057.1 (960.5) | 36486.3 (898.1) | 15273 MiB |
| 1024 | 3968.1 (1032.2) | 16778.0 (976.5) | **35979.5 (910.7)** | 15369 MiB |

The spread inside a cell is 0.2-0.5 %, and the 512/4k cell re-measured at the end
of the session gives the same 3934.0 ms, so the ordering is not drift. `omph-run
--chunk C` at 16384 tokens reproduces it independently: 16529.8 / 16854.8 /
16662.0 ms.

1024 is the fastest only at 32768 (+0.6 %); it loses at 4096 (-0.9 %) and ties at
16384 (+0.14 %), and it costs **+212 MiB** of VRAM. 768 is worse everywhere.

## Why the hypothesis does not pay: the kernels, 16384 tokens

`rocprofv3`, the same run at 512 and 1024 (total kernel time 16510 against
16476 ms, -0.2 %, 26437 against 13317 launches):

| kernel | 512 ms | 1024 ms | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `gemm_tiles2_kernel` (the prefill GEMM) | 12539.8 | 12487.6 | **-52 (-0.4 %)** |
| `attention_wmma_kernel` (prefill attention) | 1363.0 | 1315.7 | -47 (-3.5 %) |
| `delta_wy_b_kernel` / `delta_wy_a_kernel` | 461.4 | 431.5 | -30 |
| `add_rms_norm_f16_kernel` | 422.1 | 470.4 | **+48 (+11 %)** |
| `delta_conv_kernel` | 163.3 | 217.2 | **+54 (+33 %)** |
| `delta_gate_kernel`, `delta_post_kernel`, `attn_prep_kernel` | 527.8 | 530.4 | +3 |

The GEMM gains **0.4 %, not 4-5 %**: the 5120-row shapes are launched *grouped*
with their siblings, and the next tensor's workgroups fill the tail of the half
round, so the quantization the issue computed is mostly absorbed. Whatever the
bigger chunk buys (the attention's 3.5 %, fewer launches in the WY form) is paid
back by the elementwise kernels, whose per-row cost grows with the batch (the
norm +11 %, the conv +33 % at this shape).

## Numerics

The chunk moves the arithmetic: the delta-net state is rounded to f16 at every
prefill-chunk boundary, so a 1024-token chunk has half as many round trips. Two
witnesses, 16384 tokens, `omph-run --logits-tail 64`:

| comparison | KL mean | p99 | max | top-1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 768 vs 512 | 0.000741 | 0.0059 | 0.0063 | 98.44 % |
| 1024 vs 512 | 0.000812 | 0.0064 | 0.0079 | 96.88 % |

For scale: the default quantized KV costs 0.0012-0.0015 nats against f32 KV
(#175) and #240's WY form 0.0002-0.0005. A greedy answer can differ in wording -
the chat check over the same 19k-token prompt gave "serves as a design note" at
512 against "identifies the document as design notes" at 768 and 1024 - which is
the expected effect of a different but equally acceptable rounding path, not an
error: nothing here is bit-identical across chunk sizes by design.

## Decision

**512 stays the default.** The predicted 4-5 % is not there (-0.4 % in the GEMM;
+0.6 % at 32k, -0.9 % at 4k, +212 MiB), and the issue's step 4 (a 128-row tile
variant for the 5120-row shapes, or split-K over k) would be aimed at a cost that
this measurement says the grouped launches already hide.
