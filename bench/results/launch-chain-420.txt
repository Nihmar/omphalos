# #420 step 1: what fusing the decode step's launches can win, measured

Engine main 0571d06, RX 9060 XT 16 GB (160 W cap). `bench/launch/chain_size.hip`
(new): a chain of the decode step's real GEMV sizes - 64 layers x (qkv+gate
35 MiB, ssm_out 14, gate+up 72, down 39) plus the 682 MiB head, 257 segments,
10.7 GiB, against the model's 11.28 GiB in ~256 GEMV launches (#203's profile).
One launch per segment (A) against the same bytes as one launch with a
device-side queue over 128 KiB blocks (B, the fully fused ideal: the blocks come
off a global counter, so no workgroup idles at a segment's tail). Median of 5,
after a 2 s warm-up; data laid out once over a 11.8 GiB pool, so neither L2 nor
the Infinity Cache holds it.

| variant | ms | GB/s | per segment |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A: a launch per segment (257 launches) | 37.29 | 286.1 | 145.1 us |
| B: one queued launch | 35.56 | **299.9** | 138.4 us |
| C: the same chain in 2 / 8 / 32 launches | 37.24 / 37.25 / 37.27 | ~286 | ~145.0 |

**The memory-side prize of a fully fused step is A - B = 1.72 ms (4.6 % of A)**,
i.e. ~6.7 us per launch of fill and drain at these sizes - the per-launch cost
`stream_size.hip` measured (~3-11 us at 13-36 MiB), now confirmed end to end on
the step's own mix.

## Why this settles the megakernel's size

- #203 estimated ~6 ms per step from the same data ("GEMV fill/drain ~3.6 ms +
  idle 2.5 ms"). The fill/drain part is **1.7 ms**: the earlier estimate charged
  the whole gap between the GEMVs' 41.2 ms and the 35.4 ms bandwidth bound to the
  launches, but **a pure streaming chain of the same sizes already loses 3.8 ms
  to them** (37.29 ms against the 33.5 ms the bytes need at 318 GB/s): most of
  that gap is the sizes' own inefficiency (many 14-39 MiB segments do not reach
  318 GB/s even with no other kernel in flight), which fusing cannot remove -
  B is still 300 GB/s, not 318.
- The engine's real GEMV chain (41.2 ms for the step's weights) is therefore
  only ~2 ms off a *pure streaming read* of the same sizes (37.3 ms, with no
  dequant, no dots, no f16 staging).
- The other launch cost is the ~165 small kernels at ~15 us each (~2.5 ms,
  #203's note "dominated by per-launch latency, not work"). A megakernel would
  have to absorb those too, and their fusion was already measured: folding the
  add + norm into the output GEMV's last workgroup was 0.2 ms *slower* and the
  never-taken epilogue cost ~1.1 ms through the GEMVs (#103).

## Decision (for the maintainer)

The fully fused step's memory-side ceiling is **~1.7 ms** (plus up to ~2.5 ms if
every small kernel's launch latency is also removed, at the measured cost of the
epilogue fusions), on a plain step of ~42 ms: **4-10 % best case**, for the
largest and riskiest change in the project (device-side barriers, a work queue,
one template instantiation per type per phase, 64 grid syncs per step).

Recommendation: **do not build it now.** If the launch overhead is to be
attacked, the measured-and-cheaper route is fewer *groups* per layer (the #255
direction: each group already merges 2-3 tensors; the remaining 4 launches per
layer are per-input) and the small kernels' fusion only where #103 did not
already measure it as a loss. Reopen this issue if a future step becomes
launch-bound (e.g. an int8 GEMV path that halves the weight bytes, doubling the
relative cost of the launches).
