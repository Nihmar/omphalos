# The prefill attention's shape for small query counts: one row tile instead of two (#450 follow-up)

Bench `omph-attn-bench` (one layer, K8/V4) and the agent turn through
omph-server, RX 9060 XT 16 GB, 2026-10-14.

## What was tried, and what it is not

The earlier idea of key splits in the flash kernel does not pay for the *large*
chunks: the kernel sits at 240 VGPRs and `Occupancy 6 waves/SIMD` (~3 workgroups
resident per CU), so a 512-row chunk is not starved of workgroups and splitting
its key range only re-partitions the same workgroup-seconds. The measurement that
decided it is the resource report, not a guess - the earlier estimate of "~2 s off
the prefill" assumed the opposite.

Where the grid *is* small, the cheap knob is the workgroup's shape: `kFaRowTiles`
= 2 shares one K/V staging over 32 rows (#173, #191) but halves the grid, which
matters only while the grid does not fill the 16 CUs.

## omph-attn-bench, per layer (us), one row tile against two

| seq | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25000, RT=2 | 1891 | 1929 | 1938 | 2061 | 3886 | 7298 |
| 25000, RT=1 | **1625** | **1627** | **1703** | 3111 | 6122 | 11795 |
| 60000, RT=2 | 4511 | 4617 | 4624 | 4835 | 9437 | 17993 |
| 60000, RT=1 | **3742** | **3836** | **3922** | 7687 | 14489 | 28632 |

-14/-17/-15 % at 16/32/64 rows, +51..62 % from 128 rows up (RT = 1 stages K/V
per 16 rows instead of 32, and that staging is what the large chunks are made of).

So the launcher picks per call - `OMPH_FA_RT1=N` (default 64, 0 = always two row
tiles) - and the geometry stays a function of the token count.

## The witnesses

**Bit-identical**: `omph-run --tokens 4160 --logits-tail 64` (the last chunk is
the 64 routed rows) with `OMPH_FA_RT1=0` and `=64` gives the same bytes. The
per-query accumulation order over the keys does not change, so this is a
scheduling-only change - no KL, no output change, and every #161/#262 identity
check holds by construction.

## The prize: the agent turn

A 60k context, appends of the turn size (median of 6, `prefill_ms` of the append):

| context | turn | RT=2 (today) | RT=1 | change |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 60000 | 64 | 232.8 ms | **222.5 ms** | **-10.3 ms (-4.4 %)** |

That is the -11.2 ms the bench predicted for the 16 layers' attention
(0.70 ms/layer x 16), measured through the server.

| context | turn | RT=2 | RT=1 | change |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25000 | 64 | 186.1 ms | 182.3 ms | -3.8 ms (-2.0 %) |
| 60000 | 64 | 232.8 ms | 222.5 ms | **-10.3 ms (-4.4 %)** |
| 100000 | 64 | 288.7 ms | **271.4 ms** | **-17.3 ms (-6.0 %)** |
| 60000 | 32 | 200.9 ms | 201.4 ms | inert (control) |

The 32-row turn is the control: `OMPH_ATTN_SHORT_TOKENS` routes it to the decode
path already (#450), so the flash shape never runs and nothing changes. The gain
grows with the context because the attention's share of the turn does.

## The shipped configuration

With the dispatch in place the bench reports the best of both per token count
(25000: 1636 / 1636 / 1713 us at 16 / 32 / 64 rows, 2063 / 3885 / 7403 at
128 / 256 / 512): the geometry is chosen per call, and every call's is a function
of its token count, so runs stay deterministic and bit-identical to before.
