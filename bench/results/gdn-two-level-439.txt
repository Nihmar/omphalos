# #439: the delta-net prefill, two-level WY split - measured and not adopted

Tree: main after #435/#436/#437 (1d0bd1c-ish) + the branch perf/439-gdn-two-level.
GPU: RX 9060 XT (gfx1200), ROCm, `OMPH_TIMING=1`, 4096-token prompt (docs+PLAN
repeated, `omph-tokenize`), `omph-run <omph> /tmp/tok_full.txt out.f32 --last-logits --gemv`,
median of 3 unprofiled runs for the wall, `rocprofv3 --kernel-trace` for the kernels.

## What #240's phase B is (the hypothesis the split tested)

`delta_wy_b_kernel`: one block per value head (48), 256 threads, 8 WMMA waves (16
value columns each), walking a 512-token prefill chunk's eight 64-token WY chunks
*serially* - the entry state chain h' = gamma_C h + K'^T T beta (V - gamma K h).
The split (issue #439) keeps that walk alone in level 1 (also storing each chunk's
entry state and D) and moves the part the entry state alone determines,
o = gamma_t q_t h_c + P D, into level 2 for every (head, chunk) *in parallel*
(384 blocks instead of 48), with the gated RMSNorm folded in so `delta_post_kernel`
(107 us/launch) also disappears.

## Result: it loses

| kernel | launches | ms | per launch | VGPR / scratch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `delta_wy_a_kernel` (unchanged) | 384 | 47.3 | 123 us | 96 / 0 |
| `delta_wy_b_kernel` (#240, serial) | 384 | 67.5 | 176 us | 224 / 0 |
| `delta_post_kernel` (#240, after it) | 384 | 41.0 | 107 us | 24 / 0 |
| **#240 total (wy_b + post)** | 768 | **108.5** | 283 us | |
| `delta_wy_s_kernel` (level 1, split) | 384 | 64.9 | 169 us | 200 / 0 |
| `delta_wy_o_kernel` (level 2, o + norm) | 384 | 76.2 | 198 us | 120 / 0 |
| **split total** | 768 | **141.1** | 367 us | |

Prefill wall 4096 tokens: 3948 ms (#240) against 3928 ms (split) - the +33 ms of
kernel time hides in the run-to-run spread; it is not a win either way.

Iterations kept for the record: (a) level 2 recomputing D (V, K, T, b in the o
pass, no D store) was the first cut - 227.9 ms with 256 VGPRs and 636 B/lane of
spill; (b) normalising per row tile instead of holding all four tiles' o values
helped (197.2 ms, 368 B spill) but not enough; (c) storing D in level 1 and
leaving level 2 nothing but q h, P D and the norm is the version measured above
(119 VGPRs, no spill, occupancy 12 waves/SIMD).

## Why (the useful part)

1. **The serial chain is not the WMMA work.** Removing phase B's two biggest
   phases (q h and P D: ~42 of its ~94 WMMAs per lane per chunk, the rest being
   K h, R, D = T R and the h' update) shortened a launch from 176 to 169 us - 4 %.
   The walk's cost is in K h -> R -> D -> h' itself: the loads of K, V and T, the
   96 `expf` per lane, and the dependency chain through them.
2. **The split is a bandwidth loss.** The entry state is 128x128 f16 (32 KB per
   head per chunk) and must be written by level 1 and read again by level 2, and
   level 2 re-reads h, q, P, D and z rather than holding them in registers:
   ~91 MB per (layer, 512-token chunk) against ~60 MB for #240's two kernels.
   The time follows the bytes (141/108.5 = 1.30 against 91/60 = 1.52).
3. The bucket's memory floors (right dims: n_vh 48, n_kh 16, channels 10240):
   `delta_conv_kernel` 42 MB -> 132 us, measured 107 (at/below the wall, L2 reuse);
   `delta_post_kernel` vo + z + out16 = 31.5 MB -> 99 us, measured 107 (at the
   wall - the re-blocking attempt of the same session measured 47.4 against 41.0,
   also not adopted); `delta_gate_kernel<4>` 36 MB -> 113 us, measured 130;
   `delta_wy_a_kernel` ~4 MB -> 13 us, measured 123 (the T inverse: 64 of its 256
   threads, ~2048 sequential FMAs each, on an LDS-staged A).

So the delta-net prefill is not a chain problem at 4k/32k; it is bytes and, in
`delta_wy_a_kernel`, the forward substitution's parallelism. The gate kernel's
blocking is the one measured win left in the bucket (see the commit that follows).
