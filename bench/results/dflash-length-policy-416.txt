## What the keep rule did

The per-position keep rates are EMAs (`Runner::dflash_observe`). At the old
weight 1/16 a tail position needed ~32 observations to fall below the 0.12
threshold — longer than most generations — so the block stayed at its cap and
the cap-15 runs behaved as fixed 15. At 1/4 the cut lands within ~6 steps:

| text, cap 15 | drafted rows | per step | steps |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| code2k, keep 0 | 585 | 15.0 | 39 |
| code2k, keep 0.12 | 510 | 12.75 | 40 |
| prose, keep 0 | 525 | 15.0 | 35 |
| prose, keep 0.12 | 474 | 12.8 | 37 |

So the rule cuts to ~12.8 on both prose and code: it does not discriminate the
text where the tail is worthless (prose: the accepted total is the same, 350 vs
351) from the one where the extra rows do not pay (code). And on prose the two
extra steps it costs (~110 ms of ~1.9 s) outweigh the ~50 saved tail rows
(~2 ms), which is why the rule loses there while fixed 15 wins.

## Decision

- The default stays **11 masks** (`OMPH_DFLASH_MASKS`), the best single length
  on every text measured (#414's table).
- The EMA weight is kept at 1/4: a strictly faster-adapting policy for users who
  cap higher (`OMPH_DFLASH_MASKS=15`), inert at the default cap of 11 (where
  the keep rates never drop below the threshold).
- A rule that *beats* a fixed length needs a different statistic: the marginal
  acceptance of position n (keep[n] - keep[n+1]) against the row's cost, not the
  cumulative one, and a step-count-aware objective. The numbers above are what
  such a rule has to beat (+5.1 to +5.6 % on two of four texts, -5 % on one).

## Correction

#414's result file said `omph-run` does not call `dflash_observe`; it does:
`Runner::commit` calls it on every verification (`engine/src/model/runner.cc:1297,
1364`) and `omph-run`'s loop calls `commit` (`engine/src/tools/run.cc:372`). The
keep rates are live in both paths.
