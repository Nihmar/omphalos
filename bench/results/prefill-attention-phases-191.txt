# #191: the prefill attention's phases, ablated on the current kernel

Engine main 1e151d0 + this branch, RX 9060 XT 16 GB (160 W cap), wikitext prompts
of **16384** and **74599** tokens (`omph-run <omph> ids /tmp/x.f32 --last-logits
--gemv`, `OMPH_TIMING`'s `prefill wall`). llama.cpp: v0.6.0 @ d81235049 (no
llama.cpp run here).

`OMPH_TEST_FA_ABL=N` (diagnostic, wrong results with valid timings) ablates the
prefill WMMA kernel's phases: **1** K staging, **2** V staging, **4** the K q^T
WMMAs, **8** the softmax (mask + max + exp2 + sum + P), **16** the V^T P WMMAs,
**32** the O rescale, **63** all, **64** the K staging's loads and dequant (its
stores stay), **65** = 1 | 64. Each ablation removes only its own work: the
barriers, the LDS traffic of the other phases and the rest of the structure
stay, so the deltas are lower bounds.

**The ablations are compile-time** (`attention_wmma_kernel<Q8, ABL>`): the first
attempt used a runtime flag in the kernel's inner loops and cost **8 % at 16k
(18061 vs 16528 ms) and 26 % at 74.6k (123342 vs 97907 ms)** through the default
path; the numbers below are from the compile-time build, whose `ABL = 0`
timing matches main's within the run-to-run spread.

## Reading

- **The K staging is the dominant item**, and within it **the swizzled LDS
  stores**, not the dequant: removing the loads and the Q8 dequant of the K tile
  changes the wall by 14 ms (-0.1 %), while removing the whole staging (same
  loads, same consumers) saves 1022 ms (-6.2 %). The stores are two 16-byte
  writes per job into `ks[ks_at(key, 2c)]` / `[2c + 1]`
  (`engine/src/kernels/attn.hip: kFaKLd 264, XOR swizzle), 512 jobs per key tile
  over 8 producer waves: an address pattern that conflicts in LDS (each 16-byte
  store spans four banks, the 16 lanes of a half-warp write the same key row)
  and whose index arithmetic is per element. This is quadratic in the context
  (-7 % at 16k, -14 % at 74.6k): every query block re-stages the K range.
- **The QK^T and P V WMMAs are hidden** (-0.2 % and -0.4 % at 16k): 64 WMMAs per
  wave per tile cost nothing measurable, so the "critical path" reading of the
  first (runtime-flag) run was an artifact of that flag.
- **The softmax is -4.6 % / -5.8 %** and is at its ISA rates (#422): a floor.
- The V staging, the O rescale and the "no V staging" cell are neutral or
  slightly *negative* (removing the V staging is +1.7 %, probably because the
  consumers then run on stale V and the pipeline interacts differently): no
  lever there.
- Everything together is -11.2 % at 16k and -30.5 % at 74.6k, so at 16k the K
  staging plus the softmax account for the whole attention.

## What was tried and what it rules out

1. **A 8-dim staging job** (one 16-byte slot per lane, so a quarter-wavefront of
   8 lanes covers an LDS phase's 128 bytes exactly, with `gqa_load_k8`): the job
   count, the scale loads and the load instructions all double and the wall
   **loses 4 %** at 16k. The conflicts it removes are not the cost.
2. **Packed conversions** in the 16-dim job (`__float22half2_rn`): no measurable
   change - the compiler was already emitting the packed form.
3. **K double-buffered with V single-buffered** (66.6 KB of LDS with both
   doubled, over the 64 KB static limit; K double + V single is 50.2 KB): the
   whole staging moves into the consumers' first phase (where the QK^T leaves
   the issue slots free) and the second phase is the softmax and P V alone.
   **Bit-identical** (KL 0.000000, top-1 100 % against the single-buffered
   build) and **+2 % slower** (16845 / 16942 / 17115 ms against 16528-16773 at
   16k). The phases are not serialized by the staging: the producers' and the
   consumers' instructions contend for the same issue slots, so moving work
   between the phases changes nothing, and the extra barriers/setup cost 2 %.
4. The **K store pattern's conflicts** are therefore not a lever either: what
   the K staging costs is its instruction count (the Q8 load, the int8 -> f32
   -> f16 dequant, the two stores, the index arithmetic) and it is already at
   what this decomposition can measure. **Nothing in the ablation points at a
   cheaper way to feed the WMMAs.**

## The f16 K cache probe (the "cheaper operand" this file pointed at)

`OMPH_TEST_FA_ABL=128` reads K as f16 for *every* position (the ring's data at the
wrong positions, right instructions: `gqa_load_k16`'s FP16 branch without the
Q8 dequant and the scale loads).

| 16k (baseline 16576) | 74.6k (baseline 97712) |
|---|---:|
| 16627 ms (**+0.3 %**) | 94489 ms (**-3.3 %**) |

At 16k an f16 K *format* buys nothing: the dequant instructions it removes are
the ones the bit-64 ablation already showed are worth -0.1 %. The -3.3 % at
74.6k is the probe's artifact, not the format: it reads the layer's FP16 ring
(2048 slots x 1024 dims x 2 B = 4 MB, L2-resident) instead of streaming the
quantized cache (76 MB per layer at 74.6k), which the real cache must do.

**A real f16 K cache would cost, not save:** the K cache reads are
`64 WGs/layer/chunk x seq/2 keys x 256 B` = 10.4 GB per 512-token chunk at
74.6k, 1.5 TB over the prefill, 4.8 s at 318 GB/s (4.9 % of the wall); f16
doubles every one of those bytes (+2.5 % of the wall) to remove instructions
worth ~0. VRAM: +960 B per position per layer over 17 layers = +0.5 GB at 32k,
+1.25 GB at 74.6k, +1.7 GB at 100k - against a project whose first priority is
VRAM.

Decision: **no f16 K cache.** The quadratic K re-reads (every query block stages
the whole key range) are inherent to the flash structure: the fix would be a
larger query tile per workgroup (register-bound: the kernel is at 236 of 256
VGPRs) or K resident in L2 (the cache is 5-20x the Infinity Cache at these
lengths), not a wider element format.

## What the numbers say about the attention as a whole

At 16k the attention is ~12 % of the prefill (K staging 6.2 points, softmax 4.6,
everything else ~1) and at 74.6k ~27 % (K staging 14, softmax 5.8). Both of its
halves are instruction-bound and at their measured rates: the softmax at the
ISA's (#422), the dequant at what a Q8 cache costs. The remaining lever is a
*cheaper operand* - an f16 K cache (memory, PLAN 13) or a dequant that produces
its f16 values with fewer instructions - not the kernel's phase structure.
